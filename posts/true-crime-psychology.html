The Psychology Behind Our True Crime Obsession

krishnapriyavengas · Sep 6 · 3 min read

Nowadays, true crime podcasts and documentaries have become very popular. Many people start watching or listening to just one episode, and before they know it, they have finished three or four episodes in a row. This shows that true crime is not just a small trend, but something that many people are genuinely interested in. But why are so many people interested in true crime? There are actually some psychological reasons behind this, and it is more than just people being curious about dark things.

Not Just Curiosity

Many people think that being interested in true crime means a person is morbid or likes dark things. But psychologists say this is not the full explanation. According to them, this interest is connected to something deep in human survival instincts. In earlier times, knowing how people got hurt or killed was actually useful information, because it helped people stay safe. So this interest is not new, it has always been there in humans, and true crime content today is just a modern way of experiencing it.

The Puzzle Element

Another big reason is that true crime content is like solving a puzzle. In most cases, there is a mystery involved, like who did the crime, why did they do it, and how did they get caught. This mystery makes people curious, because humans naturally like solving puzzles. When people are able to guess the answer or connect the clues, they feel a sense of satisfaction.

This feeling is similar to the satisfaction people get when they solve a crossword puzzle or any other logical puzzle. So this puzzle element is also a big reason why people like true crime content so much.

Safety Learning

There is also a concept called "safety learning" in psychology. This basically means that when people watch or listen to something scary happening to someone else, while they themselves are safe, it helps them process fear without being in actual danger. This is the same reason why people enjoy horror movies also. Some studies also show that true crime especially helps people, mostly women, to mentally prepare for dangerous situations, by thinking about what they would do if something similar happened to them. In this way, true crime acts almost like a safety lesson, even though it does not feel like one.

Empathy and Justice

True crime is not only about the criminal, it is also about the victims and their families. Many people watch true crime because they want to see justice being done. They want to know that the guilty person was caught and punished. This gives a feeling of relief and satisfaction to the audience.

Also, when the case is not solved, many people feel angry or unsatisfied, because they wanted justice for the victim. So this desire for justice is also an important reason why people are drawn to true crime.

Social Connection

Apart from all this, true crime also has a social side to it. Many people discuss true crime cases with their friends, family, or online communities. This makes true crime a common topic of conversation, even though the topic itself is quite dark. Discussing these cases with others gives people a sense of connection, and this is also why true crime content has become so popular nowadays.

Conclusion

So overall, it is clear that the popularity of true crime is not just because people are morbid or like dark content. It is because of many psychological reasons, like the survival instinct, the puzzle-solving satisfaction, the safety learning, the desire for justice, and the social connection with others. This is also why famous cases like Ted Bundy and the Black Dahlia are still talked about even after so many years.
